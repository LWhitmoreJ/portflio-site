TroubleCub Games • Confidential

Two Little Wounds
Plan Overview

Where things stand, and the plan from today through the public campaign in 2028.

This page is shared privately for discussion purposes only. Please don't forward the link or post any of this publicly. A few of the details below depend on staying quiet until the right moment.

The Short Version

TroubleCub Games is an independent game studio founded by Louis Whitmore Jr. It's built around one flagship project, with a long-term plan to grow it into a franchise.

Two Little Wounds is a co-op action game about two monster hunters: a big, witty swordsman and a sharp, no-nonsense rogue. They take on a routine job that turns into something much bigger. The tone is dark fantasy with real humor running through it. The danger is genuine, and the characters' banter makes it land harder, not softer.

Visually, it's built to look like a dark, stylish comic book brought to life. Bold linework, dramatic lighting, and a look nobody else in this genre is doing. The world is designed from day one to support sequels, and eventually other formats like animation or comics, once the first game earns that.

The Timeline

Everything below runs on two tracks at once. One is building the game. The other is quietly building an audience for it, in a way that's a little unusual. Both are timed to come together in early 2028.

Now – Dec 2026
Final stretch of school, groundwork begins

The project gets set up and the game's story and world get locked down. A content channel also quietly begins during this window. It doesn't look like a typical "making of" series. That's intentional, and it plays a bigger role later in this timeline.

December 2026
Graduation. Full-time work on TroubleCub begins.
Winter 2027 • Jan – Mar
Building the first hero

Work centers entirely on one character, the swordsman. Movement, combat, and the core fighting system all get built and tested here first. Nothing else gets added until this feels genuinely good to play, since every later phase depends on it.

Spring 2027 • Apr – Jun
Enemies and a real interface

Three distinct enemy types get built, along with health bars, on-screen readouts, and everything needed for a fight to actually read as a fight rather than a test scene.

Summer 2027 • Jul – Sep
The second hero joins

The rogue becomes playable, alongside a companion character and a system that lets both heroes grow new abilities the more they're used. This is also where the two characters first come together as a real team.

Fall 2027 • Oct – Dec
The level itself gets built

A single dungeon comes together, with a supporting boss fight partway through and one large final encounter at the end. This is the complete experience the game will be judged on.

Running Alongside All of 2027
The audience-building thread

This is the part of the plan we think is genuinely different from how most developers approach this.

Instead of typical development videos, which most people scroll past, the channel's real content is entertaining comedy sketches: gaming humor, fantasy parody, the kind of thing that works completely on its own with no game ever mentioned.

Two of the recurring characters in that content are secretly the game's two heroes. Over the course of the year, they take on a handful of small freelance jobs within the sketches. One of those jobs goes sideways and turns into a short multi-part story. Each job reveals a little more of who these two are and what world they live in, without the game ever being named.

By the end of the year, sharp-eyed viewers tend to start noticing a pattern and wondering if something bigger connects it all. That noticing is the point. It's what makes the eventual reveal land as something people feel they discovered, rather than something they were simply told.

A private community (Discord) and an email list are also being built quietly this entire time, so there's a direct, owned line to supporters that doesn't depend on any single platform or algorithm.

Early 2028 • Jan – Mar
Art, sound, voice, and testing

Final art, audio, and voice work get layered on. The game is then tested with real outside players who have never seen it before. Once that's done, a trailer gets cut from the finished result.

March 2028
The reveal

The final freelance job from the sketches publishes. It reveals, for the first time, that this was Two Little Wounds all along. Anyone who noticed the pattern over the past year feels like they found it themselves. A reveal built this way also tends to draw outside press and reaction coverage on its own, well beyond the existing audience.

Days Later, March 2028
Trailer and Kickstarter page go live

A short gameplay trailer publishes, and the Kickstarter page opens for the first time.

For anyone unfamiliar with Kickstarter: it's a crowdfunding platform. People pledge money toward a project before it's finished, and if enough is raised by the deadline, the project gets funded and backers receive rewards in return, usually a copy of the finished game.

Before the real campaign opens, Kickstarter allows a "coming soon" page that people can follow in advance. How someone found that page changes how likely they are to actually pledge once it goes live:

~31%
Followed the Kickstarter page directly
~5%
On the email list
~2%
Following on social media only

This is part of why the Kickstarter page doesn't go up any earlier than this. A public, searchable Kickstarter page would be a reveal all by itself, months before the real one. Waiting means the page opens to an audience that's already paying attention, instead of starting from zero.

April 2028
Kickstarter campaign launches

Thirty days. Funds the full development of the complete game.

May 2028
Campaign closes.
Summer 2028
Full production begins

Funded, scoped, and scheduled. This is when work starts on building the rest of the complete game.

How It Gets Funded

A deliberate choice has been made not to pursue a traditional game publisher. That route typically means giving up creative control and a large share of future revenue in exchange for upfront money. Instead, funding comes from sources that don't require giving up ownership of the game or the studio.

  • The Kickstarter campaign above, funded by the audience built over the prior year
  • Game industry and arts grants that fund development without taking equity or revenue, including a major no-strings grant program run by a leading game engine company, along with independent arts funding
  • Optional ongoing supporter funding for people who want to help before the campaign even launches

On pricing, the game will be sold once, at a fair, honest price. No microtransactions. No loot boxes. Ever. That commitment is part of the pitch to players, not just a business decision.

Beyond the First Game

Two Little Wounds is being built as the start of something larger, not a one-off. The world, its history, and its characters are designed with enough depth to support sequels on their own. Once the first game has shipped and found its audience, that same foundation could support future formats, potentially animation, comics, or other media. A handful of other original game worlds have grown into much bigger properties the same way.

That's a future step, not a current plan in motion. It's part of why the world is being built this thoroughly from day one.
